The GRC Engine: Methodology

Welcome to the definitive methodology guide for the TheHGTech Decoupled Triple-Framework GRC Engine. This manual covers the independent scoring logic, mapping depth, and operational utilization of the toolkit.

Critical Expectations

The GRC Engine is a High-Precision Discovery Tool designed for gap analysis. It is NOT a formal certification platform. Scores are intentionally decoupled; achieving 100% on ISO does not imply 100% on CIS, as technical safeguards are measured with significantly higher rigor.

Technical Scope & Architecture

Unlike traditional static spreadsheets, the GRC Engine uses a dynamic Control Mapping Matrix. When you answer a single question, the engine calculates shifts across three distinct security philosophies:

ISO 27001:2022

Governance-first. Focuses on the "Management System" and Annex A control presence. It values documented proof and executive accountability.

NIST CSF 2.0

Outcome-based. Maps your controls against the 6 core functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover, and Govern.

CIS Controls v8

Technically granular. 18 Controls and 153 Safeguards. This is the hardest framework to "pass" as it requires specific technical configurations.

Product Workflow

To maximize the accuracy of your assessment, we recommend following the Standard Assessment Cycle:

Phase 1: Dynamic Scoping

The first step is selecting your relevant ISO Domains. The engine will hide irrelevant controls, ensuring a lean, high-velocity audit experience. For most technical firms, Domain 5 (Organizational) and Domain 8 (Technological) are the high-impact starting points.

Scoring Methodology: Decoupled Engines

Your assessment answer ripples through three mathematically independent calculators. Each framework evaluates your response against its own benchmark denominator and technical rigor requirement.

1. The Decoupling Matrix

Unlike basic tools, TheHGTech engine uses a Framework-Specific Mapping Matrix to maintain audit realism:

Metric ISO 27001:2022 NIST CSF 2.0 CIS v8
Target Benchmarks 93 Controls 106 Subcategories 153 Safeguards
Mapping Strength 1.00 (Direct) 0.95 (High Alignment) 0.85 (Partial / Tech)
Weighting Goal ISO Management (ISMS) Outcome Delivery Engineering Rigor

2. The N/A Mathematical Exclusion

Answering "Not Applicable" ensures that your Readiness Score only reflects your posture against the controls that actively apply to your scoped threat landscape. The risk engine completely removes that control's impact from the denominator, preventing artificial inflation of your score.

Realistic Scoring Scenarios

Use these calibration results to understand the natural (and auditor-expected) divergence between framework scores.

Scenario 1: The "Best-in-Class" Firm

All 93 ISO controls are Optimized/Full.

ISO 27001 Readiness: ~100%
NIST CSF 2.0 Readiness: ~86%
CIS v8 Technical Readiness: ~62%

Rationale: Reflects that while the ISMS is complete, the organization still lacks the specific technical configurations required for the 153 CIS Safeguards.

Scenario 2: The "Process-Heavy" Firm

All 93 ISO controls are Managed (Audited but Manual).

ISO 27001 Readiness: ~80%
NIST CSF 2.0 Readiness: ~65%
CIS v8 Technical Readiness: ~24%

Rationale: The punitive CIS multiplier detects the "Compliance debt"—where a policy exists but engineering controls are not automated.

Deliverables & Output Strategy

The GRC Engine provides two distinct deliverables designed for different stakeholders:

1. The Executive Accountability Report (PDF)

A boardroom-ready document that translates technical gaps into business risk. Use this to secure budget for security tooling or to demonstrate posture to a customer.

2. The Remediation Priority Registry (Excel)

A technical roadmap for engineers. It includes "Strategic Remediation Advice" for every identified gap. This registry should be imported into your task management system (Jira/Asana) as your 90-day security improvement roadmap.
