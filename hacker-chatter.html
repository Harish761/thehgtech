Hacker Chatter: OSINT Primer

How Threat Actors Operate

Modern ransomware gangs operate like corporate syndicates via Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS). When a victim is breached, they are added to a "Deep Web Leak Site" hosted on the Tor network (.onion).

The actors post a Stated Claim proving the compromise (often attaching passports, financial data, or credentials) and initiate a countdown timer to force extortion. If unpaid, the data is dumped publicly.

Why Use Safe OSINT?

Navigating directly to threat actor leak sites using corporate infrastructure or personal VPNs is highly dangerous. It exposes your IP to adversary tracking and triggers internal SOC alerts for communicating with known malicious infrastructure.

Hacker Chatter leverages secure backend parsers to aggregate this data. You get the Threat Intelligence (Who, When, What) securely delivered to your clearnet browser.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the Stated Claim / AI Description?

Our backend parses raw threat actor drops. However, when threat actors post minimal information, our upstream providers use AI to generate a functional Company Profile to provide context. The UI explicitly separates raw actor claims from AI-generated profiles to ensure intel fidelity.

Why are the Evidence Screenshots blurry?

Threat actors regularly post highly sensitive PII (Personally Identifiable Information) such as passports or SSNs as proof. Our pipeline actively scrambles and blurs these images upstream (PII Scrubber) to ensure your research complies with GDPR/CCPA and prevents the accidental hosting of contraband.

Is it illegal to view metadata from ransomware attacks?

Tracking public attack metadata (which companies were hit via OSINT) is entirely legal and fundamental to defensive cybersecurity. Our platform explicitly blocks direct links to the deep web payload repositories to maintain strict legal compliance.
